Templates & decks

The current PowerPoint, Word and document templates. If a file is here, it's the version to use — start from it rather than from last quarter's copy on your desktop.

construction

These link to SharePoint rather than living here. SharePoint holds the copies that actually get updated; a duplicate in this repo would go stale without anyone noticing. Follow a link and you always get the current file.

Copy before you edit. Save your own copy to the right Team first — never edit the template in place. That rule is the templates folder’s own, and it is the one that keeps every row here trustworthy.

Working in a template

RuleRequirement
Start freshDownload the file each time. A copy that's been round three projects has drifted, and you won't be able to see how.
Use the layoutsBuild on the master layouts rather than pasting free text boxes onto a blank slide. That's what keeps type and spacing right without your having to think about it.
TypeMontserrat throughout. If it isn't installed, install it — don't accept a substitution, and don't switch the deck to Calibri.
ColorThe theme palette in the file is the brand palette. Don't reach for the custom color picker.
Never edit the templateSave a copy to the right Team first, then edit that. Editing a template in place breaks it for everyone who opens it next.
Don't touch the furnitureThe deck says it itself, on its own instructions slide: leave the logo, the background gradient and the accent bar alone. They're positioned to the brand's spec.
ChangesNeed a layout that doesn't exist? Ask for it to be added to the template rather than building it once in your own copy.

What good looks like

The finished artifacts the templates are meant to produce. Use them to check your own work against — the rhythm, the amount of white space, how little goes on a page.

Two things to take from these rather than the specifics: every page has one idea on it, and the type is doing the work — not boxes, rules or shading.

Device mockups

smartphone

Phone mockups need the status bar first. The tool frames your image exactly as supplied, and a phone screen with no status bar reads as fake at a glance. Composite the brand status bar onto the top of your UI image before uploading — it's the difference between a screenshot in a frame and a believable device.
